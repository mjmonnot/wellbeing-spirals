6 · Appraisal versus occupation, and the bottom line

  • Perceived replaceability carried the psychosocial costs; occupational exposure did not. Displacement risk related to lower work meaning (β = −.07), lower workplace support (−.08), lower job satisfaction (−.11), and higher distress (.16). The Felten AI Occupational Exposure score, merged by ISCO-08 occupation, was null on support, job satisfaction, and distress; its one small path (work meaning, −.07) was not prespecified and is treated as suggestive.
  • Not every exposure metric passes the same test. In a growth model with a 2020 knot, AIOE showed no pre-trend by exposure and no post-2020 distress moderation; Frey–Osborne automatability failed the pre-trend check (p < .001), so its knot terms index occupational selection rather than disruption. None of 18 prespecified resource × exposure interactions survived false-discovery control, and a causal-forest counterweight found only weak, nonmonotone heterogeneity.
  • Distressed membership costs employers before anyone leaves. Against Resilient members: +65 lost work hours per year (p = .020), +0.60 absentee days, quit intent 17% vs. 8%, and poorer rated promotion prospects. Realized exits by 2025 did not differ (9% vs. 9%), and Distressed members reported more self-rated effort (74.5% vs. 65.9%). Wage-valued euro differentials (+€999 human-capital; +€963 friction-cost) were partly compositional: a double/debiased ML pass kept the quit-intent contrast and attenuated the euro contrast after flexible adjustment for age, sex, hours, and wage.
