Employee well-being · Research companion

Losses spiral faster than gains. The feeling of being replaceable is what does the damage.

Eighteen annual waves of Dutch employees (2008–2025; 72,433 person-waves, 16,862 people). Four personality configurations found in U.S. midlife adults reappeared on a different instrument. Employees who in 2020 felt replaceable by AI, or exhausted by technology-paced work, reported lower job and life satisfaction five years later — and it was their appraisal of replaceability, not their occupation’s exposure score, that carried the cost. Among distressed employees, optimism, self-esteem, and work meaning separated recovery from disengagement. Within persons, a rise in distress depleted self-esteem more than an equal gain in self-esteem relieved distress.

2 · Which way out of distress?

About one employee in six sat in the Distressed configuration at any wave (high neuroticism with preserved agreeableness). Over a decade, most moved. The question the paper asks is not whether they left but where they went: toward the Resilient profile (recovery) or toward the Reserved profile (withdrawal with lower engagement). 3 · What the study found, in order

  • The four profiles travel. Distressed, Antagonistic, Reserved, and Resilient, first recovered in U.S. midlife adults on the MIDI inventory, re-emerged on the IPIP-50 in a Dutch working-age panel (Tucker congruence .96 after Procrustes rotation). The Antagonistic and Resilient anchors replicated inside independently recruited entry cohorts; Distressed is a partial shape match recoverable only in the pooled model.
  • Perceived replaceability and exhaustion carried forward five years. Displacement risk in 2020 predicted lower job satisfaction (β = −.11) and life satisfaction (−.11) in 2025. Exhaustion predicted all three criteria (−.22, −.31, and −.20 on positive affect). Algorithmic management reached only life satisfaction; technology self-efficacy ran the other way (βs ≈ .10). Ten of 16 paths were significant and all survived false-discovery control.
  • Distressed membership did not amplify the stressors. The a priori moderation hypothesis was not supported. Exploratory slopes were steepest among Antagonistic members, which is a description, not a rescue.
  • Resources steered the exit. Optimism (OR 1.78), self-esteem (1.62), and work meaning (1.77) per SD predicted recovery over disengagement and held up under soft-weighted, pseudo-class, and Firth sensitivities, and under direct classification-error correction in a latent annual Markov model. Workplace support was frame-sensitive; PsyCap was underpowered (n = 44).
  • Losses outran gains within persons. Lagged distress depleted self-esteem (−.14) more than lagged self-esteem relieved distress (−.08), and the asymmetry survived a likelihood-ratio test. The optimism–negative affect pair showed reciprocal paths but no reliable asymmetry.
  • Appraisal, not occupation title. Perceived displacement related to lower work meaning, support, and job satisfaction and higher distress. Occupation-level AI exposure (Felten AIOE) was null on all but one small path, and Frey–Osborne automatability failed a pre-trend falsification check, pointing to occupational selection rather than disruption.
  • Costs surfaced before anyone left. Distressed members reported about 65 more lost work hours a year than Resilient members and twice the quit-intent rate, yet realized exits by 2025 did not differ and Distressed members reported more self-rated effort. Euro translations were partly compositional under a double/debiased machine-learning check.
Design, data, and what this site does not claim
  • LISS panel (Centerdata, Tilburg University), probability-based Dutch household panel. Secondary analysis of de-identified archive data under a signed data-user agreement. No respondent-level data are hosted here; every number on this site is a published aggregate.
  • Profiles: measurement-invariant stacked Gaussian mixture on the IPIP-50 Big Five, k = 4 retained on multi-criterion grounds (entropy = .537; Mplus 8.8 cross-check, class means r ≥ .999). Modal shares: Distressed 16.6%, Antagonistic 28.8%, Reserved 36.3%, Resilient 18.3%.
  • 2020 technology modules (robotization, technostress) are single-shot, fielded during the COVID-19 onset, and predate large-language-model diffusion. They measure appraised replaceability, machine pacing, digital monitoring, and exhaustion — not generative-AI use.
  • Stressor → 2025 models are lagged associations with demographic covariates and HC3 standard errors; they establish temporal precedence of measurement, not causation, and do not adjust for baseline criterion levels.
  • Attrition: 30.6% of pre-2020 entrants observed again in 2023–2025; baseline selectivity modest (|d| ≤ 0.18), favoring healthier remainers.
  • No intervention is evaluated. The study identifies markers that covary with well-being and with recovery-oriented exits; whether changing them changes outcomes is not tested.
